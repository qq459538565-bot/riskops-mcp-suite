2. The Three Silent Traps That Break Reconciliations

Trap A: European 1.234,56 € Amounts and DD.MM.YYYY Dot Dates

Download a PDF from N26, Deutsche Bank, or a German Wise EUR ledger and two things immediately break QuickBooks Online: dot-separated dates like 03.11.2026 fail date validation outright, and European comma decimals like 1.234,56 € either throw a column error or get stripped into garbage text. Worse, UK 04/05/2026 (May 4) gets silently accepted by a US QuickBooks file as April 5 — throwing off your month-end cutoff without a single warning. Always normalize dates to ISO YYYY-MM-DD (or explicit US/EU order) and amounts to clean dot decimals (1234.56) before touching the import wizard.

Trap B: Backwards Credit Card Signs (Amex, Chase Sapphire, Capital One)

Checking statements print withdrawals as negative numbers. Credit card statements do the opposite: a $184.20 hotel stay prints as positive 184.20, and your $2,500.00 payment prints as -2500.00. Drop that raw into QuickBooks and your client suddenly looks like hotels paid them money while they wired cash to Amex. Instead of writing =-C2 helper formulas across 12 monthly sheets at 10 PM, LocalLedger has a single Flip Signs (Amex/Credit Card) checkbox that inverts every row cleanly before generating your QBO or Xero file.

Trap C: "Looks Clean" AI Conversions That Quietly Drop Two Rows

Plenty of people paste bank PDFs into an LLM or a cheap online PDF-to-Excel converter and never notice that page 4 lost two small ACH fees. You only discover it 45 minutes later when your closing balance is off by $37.14.
